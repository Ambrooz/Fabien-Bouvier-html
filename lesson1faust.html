🎯 Conductor's Focus: The phrase must flow as one continuous line. The accent falls on the very last syllable of each sense group. The silent E's are rhythmic shortcuts — they speed up the phrase. The liaisons create the legato that connects everything.

Important: In "Ne permetterez-vous pas", the E's are NOT silent — they are all pronounced: [nə pɛʁ.mɛ.tə.ʁe]. Likewise, "ma belle demoiselle" keeps all E's: [ma bɛl də.mwa.zɛl]. The E's that drop are only those in weak, unstressed positions like je → j', or internal E's before a single consonant.

The 5 Golden Rules

"In French, you don't pronounce words — you pronounce phrases. It's like a grand orchestral legato."

Rule 1

Final Accent of the Sense Group

Individual words have no stress. The accent falls only on the very last syllable of the thought group.

From Faust: "Non, monsieur !" → accent on -sieur [sjœʁ]
"ma belle demoiselle" → accent on -selle [zɛl]
General: Je ne suis pas heureux → accent on -reux
Accent: demoiselle
Rule 2

The Silent "E"

Many E's drop in speech — but NOT all of them! Only E's in weak, unstressed positions. The E's in "permetterez" and "demoiselle" are pronounced.

From Faust: "je ne suis" → [ʒə nə sɥi] (the first E's are kept)
"qu'on vous offre" → [kɔ̃ vu zɔfʁ] — no silent E here.
General: Je te demande → "J'te d'mande" (E's that drop are after a single consonant)
E's pronounced: permetterez [pɛʁ.mɛ.tə.ʁe]
Silent E: j'te d'mande [ʃtə dmand]
Rule 3

Liaisons — The Legato

When a word ends in a silent consonant and the next begins with a vowel, link them.

From Faust: "qu'on vous offre" → link the final S of vous to offre: [vu‿zɔfʁ]
"la main" → no liaison (main ends with N, which is pronounced nasally)
General: les_amis (Z sound) · grand_homme (T sound)
Liaison: vous‿offre [vu‿zɔfʁ]
Rule 4

Nasal Vowels

Sound resonates in the nose — do not pronounce the final N or M. The nasal sound is one unified vowel.

From Faust: "chemin" → [ʃə.mɛ̃] — nasal IN
"besoin" → [bə.zwɛ̃] — nasal IN
"main" → [mɛ̃] — nasal AIN (like pain or vin)
"en vain" → [ɑ̃ vɛ̃] — two nasals: AN + AIN
General: AN (enfant) · ON (ballon) · IN/AIN (pain, vin, main, chemin) · OI (moi, toi, soir)
AN [ɑ̃]
ON [ɔ̃]
IN / AIN [ɛ̃]
OI [wa]
Rule 5

The French "R"

Soft — produced by the back of the tongue against the soft palate. Not rolled, not harsh.

From Faust: "permetterez" → soft throat touch [ʁ]
"monsieur" → [sjœʁ] — soft R at the end
"bras" → [bʁa] — R followed by A
General: Rien · cœur · bras (gentle throat touch)
French R: Rien · Rendre · bras

Word-by-Word Correction

Pass 3

Sense Groups

Mark breathing points and textual dynamics. Identify where the accent falls in each group.

Groups: "Ne permettrez-vous pas, | ma belle demoiselle, | qu'on vous offre le bras | pour faire le chemin ?"
Accents: -pas · -selle · bras · min
Pass 4

Fluid Reading

She recites the entire passage as a continuous spoken score — legato, flowing, with clear sense groups and final accents.

Goal: A smooth, connected line where each sense group resolves to its final accent like a musical phrase.

✏️ Annotation System: Have her mark her score with:

  • | = Breath bar (end of a sense group)
  • Underlined word = Strong word (destination of the phrase)
  • ‿ = Mandatory liaison (e.g., vous‿offre)
  • e = Silent E to be cut (only in weak positions)
  • ◌̃ = Nasal vowel (e.g., chemĩ, beswĩ)

💡 Tip for the Conductor: Think of this like balancing an orchestra. The silent E's are the inner voices — they move quickly. The accented syllables are the downbeats. The liaisons are the strings' legato bowing. The nasals are the resonance of the brass section.
