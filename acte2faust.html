Notes de révision — Acte II

Corrections majeures :
• gaillardes [ga.jaʁd] — le "ill" fait bien le son Y (comme dans fille).
• Liaisons ajoutées (en violet) : ces + voyelle → [se.z‿].
• Prononciation des "C" : ç et c+e/i/y font [s] ; c+a/o/u font [k]. Ex. reçu, glaçons, accueil [a.kœj].
• ENT muet : les verbes à la 3e personne du pluriel (ils/elles) ne prononcent pas le "-ent" : se cassent → [ka.s], dansent → [dɑ̃s].
• EM = nasale AN : temps [tɑ̃], membre [mɑ̃bʁ].
• Toutes les didascalies ont été retirées du texte chanté.

Diction Rules from Faust

"In French, you don't pronounce words — you pronounce phrases. It's like a grand orchestral legato."

Rule 1

Final Accent of the Sense Group

The accent falls only on the very last syllable of the thought group.

From Faust: "Vin ou bière" → accent on -bière [bjɛːʁ]
"à votre santé" → accent on -té [te]
Accent: santé → -té
Rule 2

Extended Vowels (Tenue)

In lyric singing, final vowels are often held for dramatic effect — especially on accented syllables.

From Faust: "bièreeeeee" → extended final -ère [ɛːʁ]
"rivièreeeee" → hold on -ière
Extended: bièreeeeee
Rule 3

Silent "E" — When to Drop

Many E's drop in speech — but NOT all of them! Only in weak, unstressed positions.

From Faust: "je ne sais" → [ʒə nə sɛ] (kept here)
"j'écoute" → [ʒe.kut] — E drops before a vowel
E kept: je ne sais
E dropped: j'écoute
Rule 4

The French "R" — Soft Throat

Produced by the back of the tongue against the soft palate. Not rolled, not harsh.

From Faust: "créateur" → [kʁe.a.tœʁ] — soft R
"brave" → [bʁav]
French R: Rien · brave
Rule 5

Nasal Vowels in Faust

Sound resonates in the nose — do not pronounce the final N or M.

From Faust: "en vain" → [ɑ̃ vɛ̃] — two nasals: AN + AIN
"chemin" → [ʃə.mɛ̃] — nasal IN
Nasal: en vain [ɑ̃ vɛ̃]
Nasal: chemin [ʃə.mɛ̃]
Rule 6

Expressive Silence & Breath

In French lyric diction, silence is as important as sound. Breath marks create dramatic tension.

From Faust: "Ah !... la voici ! c'est elle !" — silence before "la voici"
"Non !... non !..." — the repeated negative with breath
Silence + explosion: Ah !... la voici !

Grammar Points from Acte II

Key grammatical structures in the libretto — explained for the conductor.

1

Inversion in Questions

Formal French uses subject-verb inversion — common in opera.

From Faust: "Qu'as-tu donc ?" → What's wrong with you?
"Nous ferez-vous l'honneur ?" → Will you do us the honor?
Inversion: Qu'as-tu donc ?
2

Negative Constructions

ne before the verb, pas after — in opera, the ne is often pronounced.

From Faust: "je ne vois rien" → I see nothing
"ne nous mêlons pas" → let's not mix
Negative: je ne vois rien
3

Imperative & Exclamations

Opera uses many exclamations and imperatives — they carry strong emotional weight.

From Faust: "Holà !" → Hey!
"Allons !" → Come on!
"Approchez-vous !" → Come closer!
Imperative: Holà ! Allons !
4

Future Tense

The future tense often expresses destiny and hope.

From Faust: "Nous nous retrouverons" → We'll meet again
"je te vengerai" → I will avenge you
Future: nous nous retrouverons
5

Possessive Adjectives

Mon, ma, mes, ton, ta, tes, son, sa, ses agree with the noun they modify.

From Faust: "ma sœur" → my sister
"ton bras" → your arm
"ses pieds" → his feet
Possessive: ma sœur · ton bras
6

Object Pronouns

Object pronouns (me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les) come before the verb.

From Faust: "qui m'attriste" → that saddens me
"je vous en promets" → I promise you
Object pronoun: qui m'attriste
7

Subjunctive Mood

The subjunctive expresses doubt, desire, or uncertainty.

From Faust: "que la valse nous entraîne" → may the waltz sweep us away
"que je meure" → may I die
Subjunctive: que la valse nous entraîne
8

Prepositions with Infinitives

Some verbs require a preposition before the infinitive.

From Faust: "sans trop de façon" → without too much fuss
"à danser" → to dance (after "inviter")
Preposition: à danser
9

Prononciation des "C"

ç + c devant e, i, y → [s] comme glaçon [gla.sɔ̃], reçu [ʁə.sy].
c devant a, o, u → [k] comme cave [kav], accueil [a.kœj] (les deux c font [k]).
c en fin de mot → souvent muet : blanc [blɑ̃].

Exemples : glaçons [gla.sɔ̃], reçu [ʁə.sy], accueil [a.kœj].
C → SS : glaçons · C → K : accueil
10

ENT muet / EM nasal

-ent à la 3e personne du pluriel (ils/elles) est muet : ils dansent [dɑ̃s], elles cassent [kas].
En revanche, em se prononce comme la nasale AN : temps [tɑ̃], membre [mɑ̃bʁ].

Exemples : ils dansent [dɑ̃s] · le temps [tɑ̃].
ENT muet · EM nasal

📌 Key Takeaway: Understanding these grammatical structures helps you phrase the text correctly. The inversions, negatives, imperatives, and pronouns all affect where the accent falls.
